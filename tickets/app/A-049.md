## Reported

> "the very bottom trapazoid of the ship extends past the length of the ship… the + sign on the enemy
> ship flag is to large and doesnt fit on the flag itself… on the footer of the sea chart the fight is
> still to far out of bounds to the right they should all have the same padding"

Three overflows, one root cause each.

## 1. The waterline band jutted past the hull

The board nests the waterline **inside** the hull div, so the hull's `clip-path` trims it — which is
why the board can reuse the hull polygon verbatim. React Native has no `clip-path` and an SVG sibling
clips nothing, so drawing `HULL_POINTS` again at 12pt tall put a **full-width** top edge 27pt up a
hull that is already 6pt narrower on each side by then.

Fixed by giving the band the hull's taper at its own top edge. The hull is 39 tall and tapers 0→9
(stern) and 100→90 (bow), so at `(39 − 12) / 39 = 0.6923`:

```
stern  0 + 9 × 0.6923  = 6.231        bow  100 − 10 × 0.6923 = 93.077
WATERLINE_POINTS = '6.231,0 93.077,0 90,100 9,100'
```

Both bottom corners stay on the hull's own bottom edge, so the band is now a strict subset of the
hull outline at every height. Pinned in the fixture and asserted — including that it is **not** equal
to the hull polygon, which is the mistake it replaces.

## 2. The crossbones overhung the pennant

An 18×18 cross at `bottom: 112` on a flag spanning `110 → 122`: eight of its eighteen points sat
above the cloth — two-thirds of the flag's own height, floating in the sky.

The flag is 12 tall and 26 wide, and the tattered outline bites its right edge in, leaving the left
~56% solid. The mark is now 8pt at `left: 73, bottom: 112` — x 73→81 inside the solid 70→84.6, y
112→120 inside 110→122, with 2pt of cloth on every side.

Same class of bug, smaller degree, fixed alongside: the skeleton's 16pt skull at `bottom: 72` reached
88 on a mainsail ending at 86. Now 14pt at `bottom: 68` (68→82), with its internals scaled 14/16 so
the shape is unchanged.

## 3. The dock's Fight button had no right padding

Measured, rather than reasoned about:

```
screen 375 · row box x 24 → 351 (width 327) · buttons span 24 → 375
leftPad 24 · rightPad 0 · gap 7.125
```

`flow.ts` derives spacing from `viewport.width − 2 × 12` = **351**, but the rendered row is **327** —
a second 12pt inset sits between the screen and the row that the pure geometry cannot see. Four gaps
× the 6pt difference is exactly the 24pt of overflow. A-048's `controlGap` reproduced the same error,
because it read back `control.x`, which comes from the same 351 assumption.

Measuring also exposed the deeper problem: five targets at the 64pt floor are **320pt of button**, and
a 320pt iPhone SE leaves ~272pt of row. No gap arithmetic fixes that — the buttons themselves do not
fit, and `space-between` has no slack to distribute.

So `control.width` became a **ceiling**: `flex: 1` with `maxWidth` divides whatever row exists,
equally, and clamps at 64 where the screen allows. Never `minWidth` — that was the original overflow,
because it forbids the shrinking that keeps the last button on screen.

Measured after:

| screen | left pad | right pad | gaps | widths |
| ------ | -------- | --------- | ---- | ------ |
| 375    | 24       | **24**    | 4 ×4 | 62.2   |
| 320    | 21.3     | **21.3**  | 4 ×4 | 52.5   |

### Owner decision needed

At 375pt this yields ~62pt targets, not 64; a 320pt phone gets 52.5. **The boards set 64 as a floor
for small hands**, so five controls in a 126pt band is a demo affordance in tension with an
accessibility requirement at phone widths. The honest fixes are two rows (the dock must then grow past
the board's measured 126) or the board's own two controls. Shrinking is the least-bad third option and
is flagged here rather than silently shipped as if it met the floor.
