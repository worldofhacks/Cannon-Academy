## Why

The `Harbor and Rank` board sells ship skins and counts them as trophies. **No skin system existed** —
`shipCosmeticsForCaptain` returned one fixed palette with only the pennant varying. Both screens were
inert without this: an empty shelf, and a Rank screen missing a quarter of its trophies.

Built ahead of the screens because it is the one part no redesign can invalidate: the palettes, prices
and rarities are fixed by the board, and this is model and state, not pixels.

## Where it lives, and why that matters

`src/theme/`, **not** `src/content/`. A skin is paint — the board is explicit (_"Every ship here is
paint only. None of them shoot harder"_) and the designer attached a hard rule:

> "Never put a cannon, a timer bonus, or a hull upgrade on this shelf. The moment coins buy power,
> cut the screen."

Filing skins in `content/` would list them as game data the engine reads, which is the confusion that
rule guards against — and `content/` is engine-track scope besides. A test asserts `shipSkins.ts`
imports nothing from `@engine` or `@content`, and a second asserts the record shape carries no field
that could reach damage, so a future `+2 damage` cannot be added without deleting a test.

## The flag beats the skin

Every skin carries a `pennant` and it is deliberately unused on a real ship. Board 5b makes the
onboarding flag the pennant — the mark a child recognises as _theirs_ — and a cosmetic purchase must
not overwrite the one thing they chose about themselves. `shipCosmeticsForSkin` is a separate export
for the shelf preview, where there is no captain and therefore no flag; there, the skin's own pennant
flies, so four cards do not look more alike than they are.

**Flagged for the designer:** the board did not say which wins. This is a judgement call, and it is
reversible in one line if they disagree.

## Migration

`Captain` gained `ownedSkins` and `equippedSkin` after v2 saves were already on devices. **No
`SCHEMA_VERSION` bump** — `seenCannons` set the precedent that new fields are tolerated as absent and
defaulted in `normalizeCaptain`. Bumping would have invalidated every existing save.

The failure mode here is not a crash. It is a child launching the app and finding their progress
silently reset because a validator rejected a save that was perfectly good, so every hydrate case is
tested:

- a pre-skin save hydrates with `recovered: false` and keeps its name and coins
- the starter is always owned, even if a stored list omits it — otherwise `skinOrDefault` resolves a
  captain to a skin they do not own
- an `equippedSkin` that is not owned is dropped rather than trusted
- garbage (`ownedSkins: 'not-an-array'`) degrades to the starter instead of throwing
- a full `persist` → `hydrate` round trip preserves both fields

`equippedSkin: null` means the starter, so an untouched save round-trips byte-identical.

## A test I had to correct

My first pricing assertion required prices not to be divisible by `COINS_WIN_BASE` — and failed on
prices the designer chose deliberately (60, 140 and 260 are all multiples of 20). The real property is
that the **win count varies**: a duel pays 20 at worst and about 40 at best, so `ceil(price / 40)`
must be strictly less than `ceil(price / 20)`. That is what makes arriving early feel like luck rather
than arithmetic, which is what was actually asked for.
